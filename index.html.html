Grammar Explanation

What are indirect questions?

Indirect questions are a more polite and formal way of asking for information. Instead of asking directly, we embed the question inside a phrase. They are very common in professional contexts, with strangers, and in customer-service situations — exactly the contexts you will face in tourism.

Common introducing phrases:

Could you tell me…? Do you know…? Would you mind telling me…? I was wondering… I'd like to know… Can you tell me…? I'm not sure… Do you have any idea…?
Rule 1

Word order — use statement order, not question order

In a direct question, we invert the subject and auxiliary verb. In an indirect question, we use statement word order (subject + verb).

TypeDirect (question order)Indirect (statement order)
Wh-Where is the nearest ATM?Could you tell me where the nearest ATM is?
Wh-What time does it close?Do you know what time it closes?
Wh-How long will it take?Do you have any idea how long it will take?

⚠ Common mistake:

✗ Could you tell me where is the station? ← Do NOT keep question order.

✓ Could you tell me where the station is? ← Use statement order.

Rule 2

Yes/No questions — use if or whether

When the original question has no question word (a yes/no question), connect the indirect question with if or whether.

Structure: Introducing phrase + if / whether + subject + verb

TypeDirectIndirect
Yes/NoIs the restaurant open on Sundays?Could you tell me if / whether the restaurant is open on Sundays?
Yes/NoCan you help me?I was wondering if / whether you could help me.
Rule 3

Using the past tense for extra politeness

We can make a question even less direct by using a past tense in the introducing phrase. The meaning does not change — it just sounds softer and more polished.

Example
Past tenseI was wondering if you could give us a table for two.
Past tenseI wanted to ask whether the package includes breakfast.
Past tenseHow much did you want to spend, sir?

Key Reminders

Punctuation: Indirect questions end with a ? if the introducing phrase is a question (Could you tell me…?), and with a . if it is a statement (I was wondering…).

✗ Do you know what time does it open?

✓ Do you know what time it opens?

Never use do / does / did inside the embedded clause.

Hotel Check-In Dialogue

~15 minutes

Read the dialogue. Some of the guest's questions are too direct. Rewrite the underlined questions using a more polite, indirect form. Keep the meaning the same.

Hotel Dialogue

Receptionist: Good afternoon, welcome to the Grand View Hotel. How can I help you?

Guest: Good afternoon. I have a reservation. (1) Where is my room?

Receptionist: Of course! Let me find your reservation. May I have your name?

Guest: Yes, it's Martinson. Also, (2) is breakfast included in the price?

Receptionist: Yes, breakfast is served from 7 to 10 am in the rooftop restaurant.

Guest: Great! (3) What time does the pool close?

Receptionist: The pool closes at 10 pm, sir.

Guest: Thank you. And (4) can I get a late check-out?

Receptionist: I'll check that for you. It depends on availability.

Guest: Perfect. One last thing — (5) where do I park my car?

Receptionist: The underground car park is accessed from the side entrance on Elm Street.
